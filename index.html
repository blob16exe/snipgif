How to use

  1. Drop your files. Drag in a GIF, animated WebP or short MP4 — or several at once to batch them (up to 5 files, ~15 seconds each). They appear in the batch queue; a file that cannot be read is flagged there and the rest still process. Nothing is uploaded: everything runs on your device.
  2. Pick a mode. AUTO chooses the model for you. PERSON + PROPS keeps objects held in hands; DARK SCENE handles dark footage but runs much slower.
  3. Optional: add a sticker effect. WOBBLE, SHAKE or BOUNCE, with an intensity slider. The preview plays with the effect on, and the effect is baked into every export — what you see is exactly what you download.
  4. Hit REMOVE BACKGROUND. The first run downloads the AI model once (~176 MB) and keeps it cached in your browser; a whole batch shares that single download. Then each file is cut frame by frame on your own machine.
  5. Check the cut. The AFTER panel shows the character on a checkerboard — the classic transparency backdrop — so you can see exactly what is see-through. Prefer the old view? Switch PREVIEW to BLACK.
  6. Download. Take a transparent GIF (drops straight into chats, pages and most editors), a PNG sequence (cleanest edges, works everywhere), or an alpha WebM (smallest file, best for video overlays). With two or more finished files, DOWNLOAD ALL packs them into one .zip, each in its own folder.

The story

SnipGIF exists because this channel's videos needed characters with their backgrounds cut clean — every cutout character in our Shorts comes from this page. The big hosted removers cap free clips at a few seconds, stamp a watermark, and only let you do one clip at a time. SnipGIF runs the same open-source AI models they use — u2net and ISNet, the models behind the open-source rembg project — entirely in your browser instead: no upload, no watermark, no account, and the mode picker lets you force the model that keeps props in hands or handles dark scenes.
