How to use

  1. Drop your files. Drag in a GIF, an animated WebP, a still image (PNG, JPG or WebP) or a video (MP4 or WebM, up to 60 seconds) — or several at once to batch them (up to 5 files). They appear in the batch queue; a file that cannot be read is flagged there and the rest still process. Nothing is uploaded: everything runs on your device.
  2. Pick a mode. AUTO chooses the model for you. PERSON + PROPS keeps objects held in hands; DARK SCENE handles dark footage but runs much slower.
  3. Cutting a photo? A still image is processed as a single frame — no settings to change. It downloads as one transparent PNG, ready to drop into any design or video edit.
  4. Cutting a video? VIDEO SIZE and VIDEO FPS control how much work your machine does: the original size at AUTO fps is the full-quality default, while MAX 480P at 10 FPS cuts a long clip far faster. Videos are sampled at up to 300 frames; the export keeps the clip's real duration.
  5. Optional: add a sticker effect. WOBBLE, SHAKE or BOUNCE, with an intensity slider. The preview plays with the effect on, and the effect is baked into every export — what you see is exactly what you download.
  6. Hit REMOVE BACKGROUND. The first run downloads the AI model once (~176 MB) and keeps it cached in your browser; a whole batch shares that single download. Then each file is cut frame by frame on your own machine, with a live percentage per file. Changed your mind mid-run? CANCEL stops between frames — finished files keep their cutouts, the file being cut goes back to the queue.
  7. Check the cut. The AFTER panel shows the subject on a checkerboard — the classic transparency backdrop — so you can see exactly what is see-through. Prefer the old view? Switch PREVIEW to BLACK.
  8. Download. A still image comes back as a single transparent PNG. Animations and videos: take a transparent GIF (drops straight into chats, pages and most editors), a PNG sequence (cleanest edges, works everywhere), or an alpha WebM (smallest file, best for video overlays). With two or more finished files, DOWNLOAD ALL packs them into one .zip, each in its own folder.

About SnipGIF

SnipGIF exists because this channel's videos needed characters with their backgrounds cut clean — every cutout character in our Shorts comes from this page. The big hosted removers cap free clips at a few seconds, stamp a watermark, and only let you do one clip at a time. SnipGIF runs the same open-source AI models they use — u2net and ISNet, the models behind the open-source rembg project — entirely in your browser instead: no upload, no watermark, no account, and the mode picker lets you force the model that keeps props in hands or handles dark scenes.

What you get back

Drop in a GIF, an animated WebP, a still image (PNG, JPG or WebP), or a video (MP4 or WebM, up to 60 seconds) and get the subject back with the background cut clean, frame by frame — like a rotoscope. A still image is cut as a single frame and comes back as one transparent PNG. Animations and videos export as a transparent GIF (the default — drops straight into chats, pages and most editors), a PNG sequence in a ZIP (cleanest edges, works everywhere), or an alpha WebM (smallest file, best for video overlays). Queue up to 5 files as a batch and they share one model download, one queue, one ZIP back. Optionally bake in a sticker effect before you export — WOBBLE, SHAKE or BOUNCE, with an intensity slider. The preview plays with the effect on, so what you see is exactly what you download.

Two models, picked automatically

One segmentation model is not good at every scene, so SnipGIF carries two and chooses between them. PERSON + PROPS (u2net) is for characters — the mode that keeps held objects intact: in the Short that produced the demo on this page, the character's APPROVED sign survives all 37 frames, where a human-only model would delete the sign. DARK SCENE (ISNet) is for dark footage and robots, where the first model smears the edges. AUTO, the default, inspects the middle frame's brightness and picks a side. Manual override is there for editors who already know their footage.

Verified against the desktop pipeline

SnipGIF is the browser port of the same pipeline this channel's videos are made with — same open-source models, same preprocessing. The output was compared frame by frame against the desktop pipeline on a real production GIF: the transparency masks agree on 99.9% of pixels. The browser version is not a toy approximation of the tool. It is the tool.

The honest limits

  • The first run downloads a ~176 MB AI model — one time only, then it stays cached in your browser. On a slow connection that takes a while; afterwards it is a one-time cost.
  • Phones work, but a mid-range phone will chew through a 37-frame GIF slowly. A desktop browser is the intended home.
  • Animations (GIF, animated WebP) are capped at about 15 seconds (max 120 frames). Videos (MP4, WebM) are capped at 60 seconds and sampled at up to 300 frames, with size and fps options to trade quality for speed. Still images have no duration to cap. Frames wider than 1280 px are downscaled. This is a subject-cutting tool, not a video editor.
  • GIF transparency is 1-bit, so cut edges look slightly harder in a GIF than in PNG or WebM. For video overlays, the WebM or PNG sequence stays cleaner. The alpha WebM export needs a browser with WebCodecs VP9-alpha encoding (recent Chrome works).

If it eats a prop, smears an edge, or refuses a file it should accept, that is a bug worth reporting — the tool gets better the same way the channel's pipeline did: one real file at a time.

What can it remove backgrounds from?

GIFs and animated WebPs. As a GIF background remover, SnipGIF cuts every frame and hands back a transparent GIF, a PNG sequence or an alpha WebM — the cutout characters in this site's videos were made exactly this way.

Still images. To remove background from an image — a PNG, JPG or WebP photo, product shot or screenshot — drop it in and download a single transparent PNG, ready for designs, thumbnails and video edits.

Short videos. As a video background remover, it takes MP4 and WebM clips up to 60 seconds, cuts them frame by frame and exports them with transparency for overlays — no green screen needed.

Whichever you feed it, the work happens on your own machine: no uploads, no watermark, no sign-up, and batches of up to 5 files share a single model download.
